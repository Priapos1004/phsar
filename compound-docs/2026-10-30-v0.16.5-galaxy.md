# v0.16.5 — Galaxy (running notes)

Provisional notes, rewritten by /pr. The study ran outside the repo on a prod restore
(1,964 anime) before any code; its scripts lived in a scratchpad, so the numbers and dead
ends below are the only durable record of it.

## What the study settled

**Grain.** One star per anime. A media-level fit with a strongly weighted anime key was no
better on any metric and clumped franchises into tight knots that hid everything else.

**Training set.** Fit only on anime whose main story has a *finished* media with a MAL
score (1,595 of 1,964); predict the rest (airing, announced, finished-but-unscored). Tried
the alternative the user asked about — train on everything with missing scores imputed
plus a has-score flag, so unscored anime could form their own cluster: the flag builds a
mixed "unrated island" (an announced isekai next to an unscored 1970s short) and
destabilises the sectors around it. Rejected.

**Feature vector.** Concatenated blocks, each scaled so its mean squared pairwise distance
over the training set is 1, then multiplied by √weight — so the squared Euclidean distance
is a weighted sum of the blocks' squared distances. Concatenated rather than a precomputed blended distance
because UMAP's `transform` (needed to predict) does not support `metric="precomputed"`.
Weights ("balanced", won against desc-heavy and genre-heavy on neighbour tag/theme Jaccard
and the taste judge): desc 4, genres 2, themes 2, demographics 0.7, source 0.5, format 0.5,
age 0.3, era 0.3, studios 0.3, quality 0.5.

- Studios: top-25 one-hot plus big/mid/small output buckets for the rest beat both raw
  one-hot (hundreds of near-empty columns) and buckets alone.
- MAL score + log1p(votes) as a quality block: kept at 0.5. Higher weights split sectors by
  score before content.

**Pipeline.** UMAP to 10-D (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0) → HDBSCAN sectors on the 10-D →
per-sector Ward regions on [10-D, z(score), z(era)] → a 2-D UMAP fitted on the 10-D.

- HDBSCAN on the raw ~500-d vectors called 73–100% of stars noise. On UMAP-10 it works.
- `excess of mass` collapses into one giant sector unless `max_cluster_size` caps it; n/6
  with `min_cluster_size` 25 gives ~16 sectors.
- Regions via HDBSCAN `leaf` selection were tried first: fragmentary, and they never split
  by score. Ward per sector with score and era as extra dimensions produces exactly the
  splits the user asked for ("Cheat-skill isekai (the sloppy ones)" vs the acclaimed ones).
- 2-D from the 10-D beat 2-D from the raw vectors and t-SNE: trustworthiness@10 0.94 and
  sector contiguity 0.96 vs t-SNE's 0.91 / 0.88 — and t-SNE cannot predict new points.
- Prediction: `u10.transform` → `u2.transform`; `hdbscan.approximate_predict`, falling back
  to the `membership_vector` argmax for noise; region = nearest region centroid.

**Stability.** Backtests removed the newest month / quarter / half-year / year / two years,
fitted on the rest and predicted the removed anime. With e5: ARI against the full fit
0.76–0.83 up to a year, 0.71 at two years; seed ARI 0.85–0.86. Predicted anime land
"between regions" 26–38% of the time and match their fitted sector about half the time —
hence a refit each season rather than predicting forever. A refit initialised from the
previous coordinates moves the median star 5–10% instead of 14–27%; sectors still churn
~20%, so names must carry by member overlap, not by id.

**Cost.** Fit 13–14 s on 2 threads, ~520 MB peak; predict 10 ms warm, 0.3 s cold; pickled
models 4–8 MB.

## The synopsis model, and why search moved too

Three encoders were compared inside the galaxy pipeline, then on search itself (30
targeted queries in EN / JA / JA-title / DE, 20 keyword concepts × 3 languages):

| Model | MRR EN | JA | JA title | DE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (today) | 0.50 | 0.42 | 0.07 | 0.23 |
| multilingual-e5-small | 0.73 | 0.85 | 0.60 | 0.44 |

Keyword precision is about equal (JA single keywords 0.27 → 0.25). e5-small is the same
size (118M, 384-d), so the swap needs no schema change and no extra RAM. It also gave the
most stable galaxy. mxbai-embed-large is English-only — Japanese collapses. e5-large and
bge-m3 were skipped: too large for the 4 GB VM (and the dev disk ran out downloading them).

e5 needs `query: ` / `passage: ` prefixes, and its distances are compressed — the
description margin from the v0.16.0 study comes out ≈ 0.06 rather than 0.30, so every
calibrated constant is re-measured.

### Recalibration on e5-small (local DB = prod restore, re-embedded by the app's own pass:
1,964 anime / 6,724 media / 345 notes in 236 s on the dev Mac, no row failed)

Both constants point here for this model's run (the procedures, query sets and scripts now
live in `.claude/skills/remeasure-embeddings`): the rewrite at PR time must keep these
tables.

**`SEMANTIC_MARGIN` = 0.06, still one value for both populations.** Semantic-only hits
(literal hits excluded) admitted per margin:

| Query set | 0.04 | 0.05 | **0.06** | 0.07 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| description, worst of names (shinji, izumi, rintaro, okabe) + nonsense (xyzzy, spreadsheet, tax audit, asdf qwer) — rule: ≤ 2 | 32 | 7 | **1** | 0 |
| description: giant robots / boy who sees ghosts / detective solves murders / friends start a band | 60 / 60 / 60 / 60 | 38 / 38 / 60 / 60 | **11 / 15 / 42 / 26** | 2 / 5 / 16 / 7 |
| notes, worst nonsense (xyzzy, spreadsheet, tax audit, submarine, pizza recipe, good, nice) — rule: 0 | 2 | 1 | **0** | 0 |
| notes: calm and cozy / hilarious / dull and tedious / detective story | 10 / 8 / 12 / 21 | 4 / 6 / 7 / 5 | **1 / 4 / 6 / 0** | 1 / 2 / 1 / 0 |

Description survivors at 0.06 are relevant (Grendizer, Patlabor; Yu Yu Hakusho, Nube; Conan;
BanG Dream, Bocchi). Notes at 0.05 would recover all four relaxing notes for "calm and cozy",
as MiniLM did at 0.30, but "detective story" then admits five notes that only say "story" —
so 0.06. The notes queries are the v0.16.1 set (10 own words, 13 paraphrases, 7 nonsense /
generic); the name/nonsense list for descriptions is a reconstruction (the v0.16.0 one was
not written down in full) — write it down this time: it is above.

**Encode cost.** e5 reads 512 tokens where MiniLM stopped at 128. At 2 threads on the dev
Mac, 150 real descriptions (median 488 chars, p90 1,034): MiniLM 19.3 ms mean, e5 23.8 ms;
the longest synopsis (4.2k chars) 17.9 vs 45.3 ms; a short query 10.7 vs 11.5 ms. Not capped
to 128/256: the search study validated e5 at 512, and capping would void the calibration
below. The re-embed estimate moves from ~5-9 to ~6-11 min on the VM.

**`DESC_THRESHOLD` = 0.93** (merge detection's title+desc rule). The old 0.85 had no written
procedure; this one:
- Negatives: every catalogue anime pair the rule decides (studios overlap, title score in
  [0.5, 0.85)) — 245 pairs in a curated catalogue, so distinct anime by the admin's past
  curation. Old model: none reached 0.85 (rank correlation old/new 0.69). e5: the nearest
  sits at 0.922 (two Garou Densetsu: City of the Wolves shorts); distinct franchise siblings
  at 0.90–0.92 (Gundam Wing vs SEED, Precure seasons); even two unrelated isekai at 0.889.
- Positives, the shape of a duplicate a scrape would save twice: pairs of main-story media
  inside one anime with the same studio-overlap and title band ("Dr. Stone" vs "Dr. Stone:
  New World") — 972 pairs. Old model at 0.85: recall 0.09. e5 recall 0.91 / 0.70 / 0.43 /
  0.21 / **0.13** / 0.08 at 0.86 / 0.88 / 0.90 / 0.92 / **0.93** / 0.94.
- 0.93 flags no negative and slightly out-recalls the old threshold. The two pairs the admin
  dismissed sit at 0.917 and 0.888. The rule is a secondary signal; title-alone and
  relation-link carry most detections, so it is tuned for precision.

## Names

Auto names come from distinguishing values against a reference population (the catalogue
for a sector, its sector for a region): tag share ≥ .45 with lift ≥ 1.6; dominant source
≥ .55 with lift ≥ 1.4; studio ≥ .3 with lift ≥ 2; median score ±0.35 → acclaimed /
low-rated; era → classic / recent. About 1 in 7 come out "mixed". Hand names (16 sectors,
55 regions) were written for the e5 fit and reach prod through the admin rename endpoint,
not a seed file — a fresh database must work with auto names alone.

## UI lessons from the mock-up

- 1,964 radial-gradient circles with additive blending lagged badly and read as harsh
  neon. One tone-mapped density image per view (veil + haze + cores) looks like a nebula
  and costs one draw.
- Reading the zoom through `getOption()` and redrawing the minimap per zoom frame also
  lagged; read the model's range and debounce.
- Covers appear only deep in (≤ ~11% of the span), grow with zoom, are placed once the zoom
  settles and never overlap; zoom floor 2.5%.
- The mock-up stretched the map when zooming one axis; the real map must zoom both axes
  together and keep the aspect ratio. "First aired" colouring was dropped.

## Decisions taken with the user while planning

- The search swap's re-embed window (mixed vector spaces for a few minutes after deploy)
  is accepted pre-1.0; deploy in the daytime, away from the nightly sweep.
- Refit quarterly (15th of Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, two weeks after a season ends so finished
  shows have settled scores) plus an admin button.
- New anime are placed at each update sweep's end, not after every user scrape — a seasonal sweep
  adds dozens at once and should pay one compute child, not dozens.
- Neighbours are served on demand from stored feature vectors rather than inlined in the
  map payload: always fresh after placements, and the query the recommender needs.

## The app's fit on the real catalogue (2026-10-30, local prod restore, e5 vectors)

Ported `features.py` + `fit.py` into `galaxy_compute`, reading the stored `anime_search`
vectors (the study encoded titles + a lightly cleaned synopsis itself; the app reads what
search stores). One in-process fit: 1,964 anime (1,595 trained), 515-d features, 14.8 s,
531 MB peak, 4.4 MB blob; 15 sectors, 54 regions, 11.6% between. Against the study's e5
map: sector ARI 0.86, region ARI 0.69 — the study's own seed-to-seed level. Placing 20 known
anime: 0.06 s, 19 in their fitted sector.

**Name carry floor = 0.4 Jaccard**, set by carrying the study's hand names (16 sectors, 55
regions) onto that fit by trained-member overlap: 0.3 → 15/16 + 48/55, **0.4 → 15/16 +
44/55**, 0.5 → 14/16 + 36/55, 0.6 → 13/16 + 34/55. The names 0.4 adds over 0.5 fit their
groups (Ghibli skies & mythic films on Totoro / Spirited Away / Howl; Devils, kaiju & flames
on Attack on Titan / Jigokuraku; High Fantasy Frontier on the fantasy-action sector, which
sits at exactly 0.50); one is doubtful ("Modern shounen hits" on a low-rated recent region).

**Warm refit, measured how a viewer sees it.** The first `median_movement` read 16% of the
span — but UMAP rescales its starting layout, and the UI fits the map to the viewport, so a
shift or rescale of the whole map is invisible. With both layouts fitted to the same box:
warm refit 7.3% (Procrustes 7.7%), cold refit with another seed 32% (Procrustes 17%). The
study's 5–10% holds; the summary reports the boxed measure. A warm refit with 40 trained
anime held out as newcomers: 6.9 s (numba warm), sector ARI 0.86 to the first fit, 60 of 69
group names carried at 0.4.

**When the map rebuilds, decided at review.** The first cut enqueued from a calendar check
in `schedule-nightly` (only on the 15th) and from a boot flag set when that boot
re-embedded. Both lost the trigger for good when one moment was missed: a cron that did not
run on the 15th waited a quarter, and a restart between the re-embed and the fit left the
map on the old model's synopsis block. Now one rule (`ensure_map`) reads stored state — no
map, a fit whose recorded `built_from` (model + pickled library versions) differs from
today's, or a fit older than the latest refit date — and boot and every nightly run ask it.
A library bump refits by itself instead of waiting for an admin. A catalogue under
`MIN_CATALOGUE` (HDBSCAN's cap leaves no room for a sector) enqueues nothing, so a fresh
database does not spawn a doomed fit on every boot.

The fit was also bit-identical before and after the review's refactor (placement shared by
`fit` and `place`; age tier, watch time and the anime's score now read from the app's own
definitions): every star, feature, group and summary matched on the prod restore.

The first fit through the real path (boot `ensure_map` → worker → spawned child) on the
dev backend: 19 s including numba's first compile, same 15 sectors / 54 regions / 11.6%
between.

**Serving the map (2026-11-01).** New anime are placed at the update sweep's end, not the
seasonal sweep's: the seasonal dispatcher only enqueues child scrapes, so its new anime do
not exist yet when it finishes — they land the next night, all in one child (~10 s, mostly
imports and numba's compile). `GET /browse/galaxy` on the prod restore: 174 ms, 761 KB of
JSON, 213 KB gzipped as typed rows; a column layout would be 163 KB — kept rows, since
UUIDs, titles and cover URLs dominate either way and the tab loads it once. Rounding
coordinates and scores saved little for the same reason. Neighbours: ~40 ms.

**`/ratings/coverage` sends the rated state only.** The map's My ratings colouring needs
the state the coverage tier is a view of. Sending both put a derived field beside its
source, so the tier is now derived in the frontend (`coverageTier`), beside the styles it
picks between; the backend's `CoverageTier` is gone. A response-shape change: both images
move to the same tag, which this release needs anyway.

**Discover's description rows under e5 (2026-11-01).** Not checked when search moved to
e5; checked now (`discover_rows.py`, added to the remeasure skill as a step). The model only
moves a row's semantic-only hits, and under the search's own literal test there is one in
all twelve rows ("Magic Maker" under "magic academy"): the rows are literal sets, and e5's
margin admits almost nothing past them. A first pass with a hand-copied literal test (no
`other_names`, the query as one phrase) reported 1–22 semantic hits per row — the script
reuses `literal_matches` and `description_text` for that reason. The overlap bar, counted
as browse.md counts it (the pools when either row draws at random), has twelve pairs over
— isekai/reborn share 39 anime, offbeat's random rows 8–16 — with at most one semantic hit
among any pair's shared anime. So the model moved none of them: Discover's no-repeat rule
no longer holds for its random rows on today's catalogue, whatever the embeddings. Raised
with the user as a browse question.
Query alternatives measured and rejected: "overpowered" for strongest and "aveng" /
"vengeance" for revenge each drop most of the on-topic shows; "apocalyp" for after-the-end
(9 → 15, two real post-apocalyptic shows plus franchise noise) — the user kept "apocalypse".
The rows' real noise is literal and structural: a long franchise matches through one movie
— Shin Chan, Gundam, Doraemon, Pokémon and Case Closed each sat in three or four rows
(roadmap, "Franchise mentions in description searches").

**Deploying this release.** The first boot re-embeds and then refits the map. Rolling back
to v0.16.4 is not automatic: it predates the boot probe and re-embeds only with
`EMBEDDING_REEMBED_ON_STARTUP=true` set; from this release on, rolling back between images
re-embeds by itself.

Gotchas:
- HDBSCAN's `max_cluster_size` (n/6) means a synthetic test catalogue of a few big clusters
  finds **no** sectors: every cluster is over the cap. The tests use eight small clusters.
- The migration's downgrade re-casts `jobs.kind`; two partial indexes (`kind = 'user_scrape'`)
  block that, so it drops and rebuilds them. The older enum migrations predate them.
- `alembic revision --autogenerate` refuses to run while an enum's labels differ (our
  comparator) — hold the new label back, autogenerate the tables, then hand-write the
  `ALTER TYPE`.
- Mid-session the dev backend ran without `--reload`, so the boot-time first fit only
  happens on a restart.

## The tab, from the visual pass (2026-11-04)

Gotchas, each found by the user on the running page:
- **ECharts' `labelLayout` callback is handed the label's box where the last redraw left
  it.** A shift worked out from `labelRect` therefore undid itself on the next redraw —
  every glide frame, every hover restyle — and labels flipped in and out of the map. The
  fix places each label absolutely (`x`, `y`, centred) from its anchor (`rect`, the
  zero-size symbol) and its size alone (`labelCentre`). The same callback first also
  dragged labels anchored off-screen onto the edge; now those stay off.
- **`image://` symbols stretch a cover into their box**, and `symbolKeepAspect` holds only
  for the first load: once the image is cached, a redraw resets it to the full box. Covers
  are now cut once to the tiles' 2:3 (`object-cover`, `coverCrop`) onto a canvas and drawn
  by a custom series, which only scales them. Covers shrink with the map while it zooms
  out before the re-pack, and drop the moment the view passes the cover zoom, else a
  close (✕) showed a pile of covers for a second.
- **The inside dataZoom zooms each axis on its own and cuts a window that overruns the
  edge**, so zooming out beside an edge narrowed one axis more than the other — the map
  stopped keeping its shape, "only zooming the y-axis". After every zoom both windows are
  reset to the wider span (`evenWindows`); a zoom-in never cuts, so the wider one is the
  gesture's.
- The anime page's back link carries the anime as `?focus=`; News' rows (always mounted)
  consume it first, harmlessly — no carousel matches a bare uuid — and SvelteKit's
  `replaceState` leaves `page.url` as it was, so the Galaxy still reads it.

Decisions:
- **MAL score colours by the "Top N%" rank** — the user found the absolute ramp nearly one
  shade over the map.
- **Neighbour reasons are ranked by chance** (user, after seeing "Top 46% synopsis" shown as
  a reason). On the local catalogue (1,964 stars) the 0.25 cut drops Fantasy 38%, Action
  32%, Comedy 31% and Manga 26% and keeps 74 of 77 genres; `trait_counts` costs ~27 ms warm
  (~140 ms cold) per pick — recount at fit time beside the percentiles once the recommender
  asks per item. Ranked in the backend, not the tab: the map knows genre shares only, and
  the recommender will need the same reasons.
- **Find uses the search's title match** (every title variant, typo-tolerant) instead of a
  `datalist`, whose order was the map's and whose dropdown was the browser's.
- A pick glides the camera to 4% of the map's width (covers ~61px tall), ✕ glides back out;
  + / − zoom around the pick.
- **Filters on the map** (built after the pass, with the user):
  - A sibling `GET /search/anime/uuids` rather than a flag, which would change
    `/search/anime`'s typed response.
  - No query box: a description query lights a ranked list cut at a margin, an edge the map
    cannot show, and two text fields on one map would read as one action done two ways.
  - Search's own sheet. It lived inside `SearchBar`, so it came out as `FilterSheet` — the
    "no split needed" first read was wrong.
  - No chip row: a count badge on the Filters pill (the user's call, like the bell's), with
    Clear all in the sheet. "Show results" kept for consistency with the search.
  - The colouring and the filters persist per tab (`galaxyView`), reset on leaving Browse.
  - Filtered-out stars sit in a silent series, so in a crowded area they never take the
    hover from a lit one; the lit ones grow instead.
  - Lit stars with no value in the colouring take a muted teal (`LIT_NO_VALUE`): with both in
    grey, a genre with no ratings under My ratings read as one grey cloud (user). Teal sat
    ≥ 15 OKLab ΔE from the gold, the four theme primaries and the score ramp's ends.
  - Filtered-out stars shape only the nebula's veil (`veilOnly`): weighted into the haze,
    they fogged the map grey around a handful of results.
  - The see-all page's "Show on the map" link waits: some rows search descriptions
    (Discover's moods), which the map's filters leave out.
- Review, deferred: `?focus=` on `/browse` has three readers (News' and Discover's rows, the
  Galaxy), each reading `page.url` — they coexist because a bare uuid matches no carousel.
  Routing it once from the page to the active tab is cleaner, but children mount before the
  page's `onMount`, so it needs `consumeFocus` split into a read and a strip.

## The distance: missing tags and scores (2026-11-04)

Found while asking why the badges said so little. Decomposing Black Torch ↔ Gachiakuta (its
4th neighbour of 1,963), squared distance 5.23 against a median of 10.37: themes 0 (typical
1.12) because **neither has a theme tag**; demographics 0, both Shounen; source 0, both
Manga. 34% of anime carry no theme and 66% no demographic, and an empty block made every two
of them identical there.

Measured on the local prod restore (live fit, every star's 8 neighbours and their badges
through the app's own `neighbours`):
- **A zero theme counted as a perfect match**: block distance 0 for two theme-less anime vs
  1.65 for two that *share* a theme, though theme-less pairs' synopses are no closer than
  random (cosine .813 vs .816). The zero vector also sat nearer every themed anime than two
  differently themed ones sit to each other: 27% of a themed anime's neighbours were
  theme-less (Dorohedoro → Chio-chan no Tsuugakuro, Tongari Boushi no Atelier).
- **Unscored anime** (16%, nearly all untrained) took the median score and zero votes: 92% of
  their neighbours were unscored too, and new franchises landed beside obscure low-vote ones.
- **Why 0–1-badge neighbours (20%) were close**, as shares of their closeness: absence 26%
  (themes 14, demographics 10, unscored 2), common genres below the badge cut 19%, blocks no
  badge names (format, quality, age, era) 22%, shown badges 21%. Absence was a quarter, not
  the whole story; the rest is real closeness the badges leave unnamed.

What was tried (neighbour level unless noted):
- **Mean fill**: no change (theme-less neighbours of theme-less anime 2.41× base → 2.43×) —
  every untagged anime gets the same vector, so the user's caution held: the average acts as
  a theme of its own.
- **Missing = unknown** (a missing side counts the typical distance): themes 2.41× → 0.93×,
  themed anime's theme-less neighbours 27% → 4%. For demographics it cost the map structure
  (HDBSCAN noise 19% → 28% on exact distances) — and their absence is structural, not
  missing: 12% of manga adaptations lack one vs 79–100% of every other source. Kept as zero.
- **Genres + themes merged as a plain set** (cosine): the pull only weakened (2.26×) — short
  lists still match exactly — and the separate weights went.
- **The user's design, built**: one tag list where "4 shared of 5 beats 2 of 2" — a
  pseudo-count, similarity = shared / √((n₁ + α)(n₂ + α)), α = 2 — and, on the user's ask,
  each tag weighted by rarity (-ln share, the badges' own measure), so Action + Comedy 2/2
  (0.34) counts for less than Idols (Male) + Music 2/2 (0.66). Rarity at half strength
  (exponent 0.5) was the safer map; full strength won on neighbours and on meaning ("a shared
  tag counts by how surprising it is to share"). The "theme-less sectors" it leaves are real:
  the donghua cultivation cluster (web novels, sparsely tagged on MAL) and theme-less
  fantasy LN/game adaptations.
- **Exact, not approximated.** Both the tag evidence and the unknown score have one form,
  d² = |u₁ − u₂|² + o₁ + o₂ with one number per star, which no fixed vector holds. A per-anime
  random direction (128 dims, seeded by id) approximated it well, but exact cost little: the
  score part alone never reaches the fit (training anime are all scored), and UMAP 0.5.12
  fits and transforms from a precomputed distance matrix, so no custom numba metric. The
  neighbours query orders by `(features <-> q)² + uncertainty`.
- Gotcha: a hand-written |a|² + |b|² − 2ab expansion cancelled to zero on the synthetic test
  catalogue, where one block had no spread and its near-zero scale blew the column up. Now
  scipy's `cdist` (differences, not the expansion), and a zero-spread block left unscaled.
- A precomputed UMAP keeps its whole training distance matrix (10 MB at 1,595 training
  anime, quadratic), of which `transform` reads only the row count: cut to that before
  pickling, the pickle stays 4.3 MB. The fit's own placement runs on the cut model.
- **Placement depends on its batch for weakly evidenced anime.** UMAP's `transform`
  optimises new points stochastically alongside the rest of their batch. With honest
  distances an anime with little evidence (kids' shows and obscure shorts: few tags, no
  score) is nearly equidistant from every training anime, so placed alone vs with the fit's
  batch it can land elsewhere: median 0.24 map units of ~44, but 7 of 80 moved over 3 (max
  14; the old zero-filled vectors anchored them falsely tight, max 1.6). Tried: leaving a
  row's own uncertainty out of UMAP's input (worse, max 23); placing at the transform's
  starting point (deterministic, but half the placed anime then sit between regions vs 31%,
  and a third change sector). Kept the transform; the stars refresh at each refit.
  (Resolved the next day: each anime is placed alone — below.)
- **Sector counts vary run to run** (the study's seed ARI ~0.86): swapping the expansion for
  `cdist` changed the float bits and the same seed gave 13 sectors instead of 15 (Romance ·
  Comedy merged into a "mixed" one). A warm refit from the approved map with the final code:
  13 sectors (approved map 16), between 10%, median movement 5.6%, sector ARI 0.80.
- **More, smaller sectors are less stable, not more** (user's question: would in-between
  sectors steady the big ones?). HDBSCAN swept on the 10-D layouts of three seeds: today's
  (min size 25, min samples 5, cap n/6) 13 sectors, between 11%, seed ARI 0.85, a sector's
  best-match Jaccard in another seed (size-weighted) 0.84; min size 15 → 20–35 sectors,
  ARI 0.76–0.83, best match 0.72–0.77, between up to 28% with a tighter cap; leaf selection
  ARI 0.75, between 35–38%. Smaller sectors are more homogeneous in tags (+0.17–0.19 tag
  Jaccard over random vs +0.14) but every extra boundary is one more a rerun draws
  differently. Coarser (min size 40, min samples 10) is the stable direction: 10 sectors,
  ARI 0.95, best match 0.93, tag lift +0.13. Kept today's; regions are the finer layer.

Old features → new, on the real catalogue: 0–1-badge neighbours 19.7% → 11.3%, badges per
neighbour 2.62 → 2.87, synopsis agreement (percentile) 81.9 → 84.3, themed anime's theme-less
neighbours 27% → 15% and those sharing a theme 56% → 62%; 15 sectors / 52 regions (was 54),
between 12% → 17% (the study's pick had 17%); seed ARI 0.89/0.91 → 0.86/0.89; a year on,

## Missing values and placement (2026-11-05)

Nothing of the galaxy was in prod yet, so the uncertainty column was folded into the galaxy
tables migration rather than kept as a second one.

What is missing, trained vs placed anime: no watch time at all 0% / 17%, no studio 2% /
33%, no source 1% / 4%, no age rating 0% / 4%, no year 0% / 0%, no score 0% / 83%, **no
synopsis 1% / 28%**. One rule now covers them all (how well each value is known, in `_raw_blocks`; the shrink, in
`_blocks`): a value known to degree c
shrinks toward the training anime's mean by √c and carries (1 − c) of the spread as
uncertainty, so an absent value is the mean plus the whole spread, as the score was. Tags
keep the pseudo-count; demographics stay zero (their absence follows the source).
- Studio, source, watch time, age on their own: placed anime's placed neighbours 61% → 57%.
  Small, kept for the one rule.
- **The empty synopsis was the real pull**: the stored vector embeds the titles alone, short
  texts sit close together, and 88% of an empty-synopsis anime's neighbours had none either
  (base 6%). Unknown → 47%; counted by length, c = L/(L + 50) → 45%, the neighbours' tag
  Jaccard .42 → .51. The mean embedding is the centre to shrink toward, not zero: random
  synopses already sit at cosine ~0.82, so a zero-shrunk short one would land beyond every
  typical pair. Half-lengths 30 / 50 / 70 were near-identical (synopsis agreement 83.4 /
  83.1 / 82.9); larger ones shift influence from synopsis to tags for everyone.
- **Filling from similar anime (user's question) measured and rejected.** How much the known
  blocks predict each value (5-fold R², kNN on the training anime): score .23, log votes .55,
  age .32, watch time from the media type .04. Mostly weak, and it counts the predictor
  twice — two unscored anime filled from the same genres come out alike in score *because*
  they share genres, which the tag block already rewards — and placement would need the
  model too. The catalogue mean plus spread is honest about what is unknown.
- Studios as a rarity- and evidence-weighted set (instead of the top 25 plus size buckets):
  21% of neighbour lists change, no measurable gain. Not taken.
- Which block orders each star's 50 nearest candidates (share of the spread): tags 39%,
  synopsis 21%, source 10%, demographics 9%, format 7%, quality 5%, studios 4%, age 3%, era
  2% — equal weights are not equal influence, synopsis distances bunch together. Kept as is
  (user's call).
- **Placement one anime at a time**: about 7 ms each (unpickle included), a few seconds a
  fit, and a star's spot no longer depends on what was placed beside it.

Results: empty-synopsis anime's empty neighbours 88% → 45%, placed anime's placed neighbours
61% → 50%, themed anime sharing a theme 62% → 64%, 0–1-badge neighbours 11.3% → 12.3% (the
price of not trusting title-only synopses); cold fit 12 sectors / 54 regions, between 12%,
seed ARI 0.90, pickle 4.3 MB; a warm refit from the approved map 14 sectors, movement 11%.
The user approved the rebuilt map (14 sectors, between 11%). A warm refit from *that* map:
14 sectors, movement 7% — but between 31%. The between share swings run to run (19% → 10%
and 11% → 31% on two warm refits): HDBSCAN's hard noise label is sensitive to the layout.
Open: a "between" read off how sure the soft membership is rather than the noise label.
- The synopsis badge had the same root cause: two anime without a synopsis compared their
  titles-only embeddings. Now a synopsis is shared only between two that have one, and the
  percentiles are taken over such pairs.

## The width pass (2026-11-07)

Gotchas:
- **ECharts' `shiftY` is a one-column algorithm** (`shiftLayoutOnXY`): it sorts every label
  by y and pushes each below the last, whatever their x, and only downward. Names side by
  side pushed each other off their clusters. Replaced by `spreadLabels` (its doc says how).
  Sizes are measured beforehand with `echarts.graphic.Text`, which matches the drawn box
  once the outline is passed as `stroke`/`lineWidth`; the solve runs once per layout pass,
  keyed on the view, ~0.1 ms at the region level's usual 15–25 names on screen.
- **`hideOverlap` ranks by the host symbol's area, then by the order ECharts walks the
  series' elements**, which is not the data order (read off the live instance: Comedy,
  Romance, Martial Arts, …). Sorting the data did nothing; a sub-pixel `symbolSize` per name
  carries the rank.
- **Hot reload never swaps the chart's callbacks**: the `labelLayout` closures stay the old
  component's until the page reloads, so a layout change looks unapplied.

Decisions (user):
- Below `sm`, and on a map under 420px tall (only a short screen), names hide on collision,
  biggest group first and the spotlit one above all; wider maps spread. Phone names are
  12 / 10.5px, wrapping at 130 / 110px.
- Sector names hide once region names show, at every width: faded, they layered under the
  region names.
- The empty panel lists the sectors with their counts, at every width: a phone has no hover
  to explore the map with.
- The overview marks the picked anime in the spotlight's cyan, larger.
- The map's floor is `min(420px, 100svh − 6rem)`, so a landscape phone sees it whole with
  page left to scroll by — a drag on the map pans it.

## The update sweep's weekly window: 5 → 3 years (2026-11-07)

Not galaxy, but this release. On prod the update sweep averaged ≈ 483 media a night against
`JOBS_SWEEP_MAX_PER_RUN` = 500, plus 67 stabilizing, so the cap already cut some nights;
≈ 238 of the 483 came from the weekly cycle (main entries premiered within
`SWEEP_RECENT_MAIN_YEARS`).

Evidence, from the local prod dump: 60 days of `update_sweep` `result_summary.media_changes`
(Aug 31 – Oct 29), finished main entries that predate the window. "Real change" is anything
but vote counts, score drift under 0.05, cover .jpg/.webp flips and the
mal_url/duration_seconds corrections.

| age | real change / 60d | score moved ≥ 0.05 |
|---|---|---|
| 0–0.5y | 56% | 38% |
| 0.5–1y | 16% | 3.7% |
| 1–3y | 6–9% | 0–2.5% |
| 3–5y | 1.4–3.8% | 0% |

- Scores settle within the first year: past 6 months, 90% drift by 0.01 or less in 60 days.
  The 3–5y changes were all minor (a description filled in, a title tweak, a genre added,
  studio edits).
- **Why 3, not 1–2:** step 2's relations probe runs only for anime with a media due that
  night, so the window also paces sequel discovery. It found 3 newly announced sequels on
  franchises 2.0–2.4 years old, and only back-catalogue filler at 3–5 years.
- Effect on the dev DB: the weekly cycle's stabilized members 1,295 → 937, ≈ 47 fewer media a
  night; prod's tier is ≈ 1.3× larger, so ≈ 60 fewer (≈ 483 → 420). Backfills of 2021–2023
  seasons now land in the 90-day cycle.
- **It stays a module constant.** The v0.14.0 doc deferred promoting it to settings until data
  showed the cutoff wrong. Data did, but it argued one new value for every deployment, not a
  per-deploy knob, so the constant changed instead.

## Name anchors move into the fit (2026-11-07)

The browser placed each name on its group's densest cluster (`labelAnchors`), because the
group's stored x, y — its trained members' mean — sat between a split sector's parts. Agreed
with the user: the stored position should be the right spot, so `galaxy_compute._group`
computes it with the same rule (`ANCHOR_RADIUS` 0.04 of the trained layout's extent, placed
anime never counting), `FITTING` 2 → 3 refits, and the tab reads x, y as given. The browser
keeps only presentation: the shift apart on overlap and the inward slide at the edge.
`sectorHues` reads the same x, y, so the colouring follows the anchors; checked on the
rebuilt map with the names at 375 and 1024 (user).
