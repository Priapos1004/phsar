# v0.16.5 — Galaxy (running notes)

Provisional notes, rewritten by /pr. The study ran outside the repo on a prod restore
(1,964 anime) before any code; its scripts lived in a scratchpad, so the numbers and dead
ends below are the only durable record of it.

## What the study settled

**Grain.** One star per anime. A media-level fit with a strongly weighted anime key was no
better on any metric and clumped franchises into tight knots that hid everything else.

**Training set.** Fit only on anime whose main story has a *finished* media with a MAL
score (1,595 of 1,964); predict the rest (airing, announced, finished-but-unscored). Tried
the alternative the user asked about — train on everything with missing scores imputed
plus a has-score flag, so unscored anime could form their own cluster: the flag builds a
mixed "unrated island" (an announced isekai next to an unscored 1970s short) and
destabilises the sectors around it. Rejected.

**Feature vector.** Concatenated blocks, each scaled so its mean squared pairwise distance
over the training set is 1, then multiplied by √weight — so the squared Euclidean distance
is a weighted sum of the blocks' squared distances. Concatenated rather than a precomputed blended distance
because UMAP's `transform` (needed to predict) does not support `metric="precomputed"`.
Weights ("balanced", won against desc-heavy and genre-heavy on neighbour tag/theme Jaccard
and the taste judge): desc 4, genres 2, themes 2, demographics 0.7, source 0.5, format 0.5,
age 0.3, era 0.3, studios 0.3, quality 0.5.

- Studios: top-25 one-hot plus big/mid/small output buckets for the rest beat both raw
  one-hot (hundreds of near-empty columns) and buckets alone.
- MAL score + log1p(votes) as a quality block: kept at 0.5. Higher weights split sectors by
  score before content.

**Pipeline.** UMAP to 10-D (n_neighbors 15, min_dist 0) → HDBSCAN sectors on the 10-D →
per-sector Ward regions on [10-D, z(score), z(era)] → a 2-D UMAP fitted on the 10-D.

- HDBSCAN on the raw ~500-d vectors called 73–100% of stars noise. On UMAP-10 it works.
- `excess of mass` collapses into one giant sector unless `max_cluster_size` caps it; n/6
  with `min_cluster_size` 25 gives ~16 sectors.
- Regions via HDBSCAN `leaf` selection were tried first: fragmentary, and they never split
  by score. Ward per sector with score and era as extra dimensions produces exactly the
  splits the user asked for ("Cheat-skill isekai (the sloppy ones)" vs the acclaimed ones).
- 2-D from the 10-D beat 2-D from the raw vectors and t-SNE: trustworthiness@10 0.94 and
  sector contiguity 0.96 vs t-SNE's 0.91 / 0.88 — and t-SNE cannot predict new points.
- Prediction: `u10.transform` → `u2.transform`; `hdbscan.approximate_predict`, falling back
  to the `membership_vector` argmax for noise; region = nearest region centroid.

**Stability.** Backtests removed the newest month / quarter / half-year / year / two years,
fitted on the rest and predicted the removed anime. With e5: ARI against the full fit
0.76–0.83 up to a year, 0.71 at two years; seed ARI 0.85–0.86. Predicted anime land
"between regions" 26–38% of the time and match their fitted sector about half the time —
hence a refit each season rather than predicting forever. A refit initialised from the
previous coordinates moves the median star 5–10% instead of 14–27%; sectors still churn
~20%, so names must carry by member overlap, not by id.

**Cost.** Fit 13–14 s on 2 threads, ~520 MB peak; predict 10 ms warm, 0.3 s cold; pickled
models 4–8 MB.

## The synopsis model, and why search moved too

Three encoders were compared inside the galaxy pipeline, then on search itself (30
targeted queries in EN / JA / JA-title / DE, 20 keyword concepts × 3 languages):

| Model | MRR EN | JA | JA title | DE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (today) | 0.50 | 0.42 | 0.07 | 0.23 |
| multilingual-e5-small | 0.73 | 0.85 | 0.60 | 0.44 |

Keyword precision is about equal (JA single keywords 0.27 → 0.25). e5-small is the same
size (118M, 384-d), so the swap needs no schema change and no extra RAM. It also gave the
most stable galaxy. mxbai-embed-large is English-only — Japanese collapses. e5-large and
bge-m3 were skipped: too large for the 4 GB VM (and the dev disk ran out downloading them).

e5 needs `query: ` / `passage: ` prefixes, and its distances are compressed — the
description margin from the v0.16.0 study comes out ≈ 0.06 rather than 0.30, so every
calibrated constant is re-measured.

### Recalibration on e5-small (local DB = prod restore, re-embedded by the app's own pass:
1,964 anime / 6,724 media / 345 notes in 236 s on the dev Mac, no row failed)

Both constants point here for this model's run (the procedures, query sets and scripts now
live in `.claude/skills/remeasure-embeddings`): the rewrite at PR time must keep these
tables.

**`SEMANTIC_MARGIN` = 0.06, still one value for both populations.** Semantic-only hits
(literal hits excluded) admitted per margin:

| Query set | 0.04 | 0.05 | **0.06** | 0.07 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| description, worst of names (shinji, izumi, rintaro, okabe) + nonsense (xyzzy, spreadsheet, tax audit, asdf qwer) — rule: ≤ 2 | 32 | 7 | **1** | 0 |
| description: giant robots / boy who sees ghosts / detective solves murders / friends start a band | 60 / 60 / 60 / 60 | 38 / 38 / 60 / 60 | **11 / 15 / 42 / 26** | 2 / 5 / 16 / 7 |
| notes, worst nonsense (xyzzy, spreadsheet, tax audit, submarine, pizza recipe, good, nice) — rule: 0 | 2 | 1 | **0** | 0 |
| notes: calm and cozy / hilarious / dull and tedious / detective story | 10 / 8 / 12 / 21 | 4 / 6 / 7 / 5 | **1 / 4 / 6 / 0** | 1 / 2 / 1 / 0 |

Description survivors at 0.06 are relevant (Grendizer, Patlabor; Yu Yu Hakusho, Nube; Conan;
BanG Dream, Bocchi). Notes at 0.05 would recover all four relaxing notes for "calm and cozy",
as MiniLM did at 0.30, but "detective story" then admits five notes that only say "story" —
so 0.06. The notes queries are the v0.16.1 set (10 own words, 13 paraphrases, 7 nonsense /
generic); the name/nonsense list for descriptions is a reconstruction (the v0.16.0 one was
not written down in full) — write it down this time: it is above.

**Encode cost.** e5 reads 512 tokens where MiniLM stopped at 128. At 2 threads on the dev
Mac, 150 real descriptions (median 488 chars, p90 1,034): MiniLM 19.3 ms mean, e5 23.8 ms;
the longest synopsis (4.2k chars) 17.9 vs 45.3 ms; a short query 10.7 vs 11.5 ms. Not capped
to 128/256: the search study validated e5 at 512, and capping would void the calibration
below. The re-embed estimate moves from ~5-9 to ~6-11 min on the VM.

**`DESC_THRESHOLD` = 0.93** (merge detection's title+desc rule). The old 0.85 had no written
procedure; this one:
- Negatives: every catalogue anime pair the rule decides (studios overlap, title score in
  [0.5, 0.85)) — 245 pairs in a curated catalogue, so distinct anime by the admin's past
  curation. Old model: none reached 0.85 (rank correlation old/new 0.69). e5: the nearest
  sits at 0.922 (two Garou Densetsu: City of the Wolves shorts); distinct franchise siblings
  at 0.90–0.92 (Gundam Wing vs SEED, Precure seasons); even two unrelated isekai at 0.889.
- Positives, the shape of a duplicate a scrape would save twice: pairs of main-story media
  inside one anime with the same studio-overlap and title band ("Dr. Stone" vs "Dr. Stone:
  New World") — 972 pairs. Old model at 0.85: recall 0.09. e5 recall 0.91 / 0.70 / 0.43 /
  0.21 / **0.13** / 0.08 at 0.86 / 0.88 / 0.90 / 0.92 / **0.93** / 0.94.
- 0.93 flags no negative and slightly out-recalls the old threshold. The two pairs the admin
  dismissed sit at 0.917 and 0.888. The rule is a secondary signal; title-alone and
  relation-link carry most detections, so it is tuned for precision.

## Names

Auto names come from distinguishing values against a reference population (the catalogue
for a sector, its sector for a region): tag share ≥ .45 with lift ≥ 1.6; dominant source
≥ .55 with lift ≥ 1.4; studio ≥ .3 with lift ≥ 2; median score ±0.35 → acclaimed /
low-rated; era → classic / recent. About 1 in 7 come out "mixed". Hand names (16 sectors,
55 regions) were written for the e5 fit and reach prod through the admin rename endpoint,
not a seed file — a fresh database must work with auto names alone.

## UI lessons from the mock-up

- 1,964 radial-gradient circles with additive blending lagged badly and read as harsh
  neon. One tone-mapped density image per view (veil + haze + cores) looks like a nebula
  and costs one draw.
- Reading the zoom through `getOption()` and redrawing the minimap per zoom frame also
  lagged; read the model's range and debounce.
- Covers appear only deep in (≤ ~11% of the span), grow with zoom, are placed once the zoom
  settles and never overlap; zoom floor 2.5%.
- The mock-up stretched the map when zooming one axis; the real map must zoom both axes
  together and keep the aspect ratio. "First aired" colouring was dropped.

## Decisions taken with the user while planning

- The search swap's re-embed window (mixed vector spaces for a few minutes after deploy)
  is accepted pre-1.0; deploy in the daytime, away from the nightly sweep.
- Refit quarterly (15th of Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, two weeks after a season ends so finished
  shows have settled scores) plus an admin button.
- New anime are placed at each sweep's end, not after every user scrape — a seasonal sweep
  adds dozens at once and should pay one compute child, not dozens.
- Neighbours are served on demand from stored feature vectors rather than inlined in the
  map payload: always fresh after placements, and the query the recommender needs.

## The app's fit on the real catalogue (2026-10-30, local prod restore, e5 vectors)

Ported `features.py` + `fit.py` into `galaxy_compute`, reading the stored `anime_search`
vectors (the study encoded titles + a lightly cleaned synopsis itself; the app reads what
search stores). One in-process fit: 1,964 anime (1,595 trained), 515-d features, 14.8 s,
531 MB peak, 4.4 MB blob; 15 sectors, 54 regions, 11.6% between. Against the study's e5
map: sector ARI 0.86, region ARI 0.69 — the study's own seed-to-seed level. Placing 20 known
anime: 0.06 s, 19 in their fitted sector.

**Name carry floor = 0.4 Jaccard**, set by carrying the study's hand names (16 sectors, 55
regions) onto that fit by trained-member overlap: 0.3 → 15/16 + 48/55, **0.4 → 15/16 +
44/55**, 0.5 → 14/16 + 36/55, 0.6 → 13/16 + 34/55. The names 0.4 adds over 0.5 fit their
groups (Ghibli skies & mythic films on Totoro / Spirited Away / Howl; Devils, kaiju & flames
on Attack on Titan / Jigokuraku; High Fantasy Frontier on the fantasy-action sector, which
sits at exactly 0.50); one is doubtful ("Modern shounen hits" on a low-rated recent region).

**Warm refit, measured how a viewer sees it.** The first `median_movement` read 16% of the
span — but UMAP rescales its starting layout, and the UI fits the map to the viewport, so a
shift or rescale of the whole map is invisible. With both layouts fitted to the same box:
warm refit 7.3% (Procrustes 7.7%), cold refit with another seed 32% (Procrustes 17%). The
study's 5–10% holds; the summary reports the boxed measure. A warm refit with 40 trained
anime held out as newcomers: 6.9 s (numba warm), sector ARI 0.86 to the first fit, 60 of 69
group names carried at 0.4.

**When the map rebuilds, decided at review.** The first cut enqueued from a calendar check
in `schedule-nightly` (only on the 15th) and from a boot flag set when that boot
re-embedded. Both lost the trigger for good when one moment was missed: a cron that did not
run on the 15th waited a quarter, and a restart between the re-embed and the fit left the
map on the old model's synopsis block. Now one rule (`ensure_map`) reads stored state — no
map, a fit whose recorded `built_from` (model + pickled library versions) differs from
today's, or a fit older than the latest refit date — and boot and every nightly run ask it.
A library bump refits by itself instead of waiting for an admin. A catalogue under
`MIN_CATALOGUE` (HDBSCAN's cap leaves no room for a sector) enqueues nothing, so a fresh
database does not spawn a doomed fit on every boot.

The fit was also bit-identical before and after the review's refactor (placement shared by
`fit` and `place`; age tier, watch time and the anime's score now read from the app's own
definitions): every star, feature, group and summary matched on the prod restore.

The first fit through the real path (boot `ensure_map` → worker → spawned child) on the
dev backend: 19 s including numba's first compile, same 15 sectors / 54 regions / 11.6%
between.

Gotchas:
- HDBSCAN's `max_cluster_size` (n/6) means a synthetic test catalogue of a few big clusters
  finds **no** sectors: every cluster is over the cap. The tests use eight small clusters.
- The migration's downgrade re-casts `jobs.kind`; two partial indexes (`kind = 'user_scrape'`)
  block that, so it drops and rebuilds them. The older enum migrations predate them.
- `alembic revision --autogenerate` refuses to run while an enum's labels differ (our
  comparator) — hold the new label back, autogenerate the tables, then hand-write the
  `ALTER TYPE`.
- Mid-session the dev backend ran without `--reload`, so the boot-time first fit only
  happens on a restart.
