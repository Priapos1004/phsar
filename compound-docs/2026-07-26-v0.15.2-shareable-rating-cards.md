## Summary
Adds a share icon beside the watchlist bookmark on the anime and media heroes that exports a
**1080×1350 PNG** on the user's active theme gradient. **Two variants off one shell**, chosen
by whether a rating exists and never by account type:

- **rating card** — cover, title, score, the 5-axis quality radar and the 6 descriptive pills;
- **info card** — airing + story-complete badges, catalog facts, genre/age/studio chips and the
  synopsis where the radar would be.

A rated viewer gets a toggle between them; each variant is built at most once per dialog
session. **Entirely frontend**: no endpoint, no migration, no stored state, and no image proxy.
Also fixes two shared attribute-viz behaviours the card exposed (the `not_applicable` sentinel
rendering as a value; the radar disappearing while its pills stayed), which changes the anime
page too, and two share-sheet defects (a false error on a second Share click; "Save image"
being unable to reach the iPhone photo library).

## Key decisions (rationale not obvious from the code)

### Shape of the feature

- **An image, not a hosted share link.** The obvious design was rejected on privacy and
  durability grounds: a temporary link rots (the thing you sent a friend stops working), and
  a permanent one is a public endpoint that can be enumerated or scraped to harvest users'
  rating data. A PNG the user owns is permanent, works in every messenger, and needs no
  public surface, no share-state table, and no expiry policy.
- **`cdn.myanimelist.net` sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`** — verified empirically
  with `curl -H "Origin: http://localhost:5173" -D -` before committing to an approach. This
  was *the* load-bearing unknown: it means a cover can be inlined into the capture without
  tainting the canvas, so the backend image proxy this would otherwise have needed **does not
  exist**. Everything else the card shows is already in page state, so the feature makes zero
  API calls.
- **A purpose-built card at a fixed pixel size, not a screenshot of the page.** Screenshotting
  would inherit the page's responsive layout, so the same rating would export differently from
  a phone than from a desktop. The card is laid out at exactly half the output (540×675) so
  the capture scale is an integer 2 — no fractional device pixels smearing text.
- **The card is deliberately the app's own visual sandwich** (themed page gradient behind a
  white elevated card). That isn't only aesthetic: it's *why* `AttributeRadar` and
  `AttributeBadges` render here unchanged — their colours are already tuned for that white
  surface — so the card and the on-page Attribute Summary stay one implementation.
- **The dialog previews the generated PNG, not the live card.** The rasterizer's foreignObject
  pass can differ subtly from live DOM; previewing the actual bytes makes any such difference
  visible in the dialog instead of in someone's chat, and makes Save/Share instant.
- **Notes are never on the card**, and clipboard-copy was skipped. A note is the most personal
  rating field and can carry spoilers; sharing a score + attribute profile is a different act
  from publishing your write-up.

### What the cards say

- **One content builder per grain and variant** (`animeRatingCard` / `mediaRatingCard` /
  `animeInfoCard` / `mediaInfoCard` → a whole `ShareVariantContent`) rather than per-field
  derivations in the wrappers. Returning the whole shape is what makes the one-entry-anime
  case atomic: an earlier cut keyed the cover on "is there one aired media" and the text on
  "…and is it rated", so the two could disagree and render a film's cover beside franchise
  counts. Assembling entire cards in the pure module (rather than in the components) is also
  what let the two grain wrappers shrink to four derived lines each.
- **The variant is chosen by whether a rating exists, never by account type.** Guests can't
  rate, so they fall into the unrated case for free — no `restricted` prop, no guest branch,
  nothing to keep in sync with the role system. It also means the feature's most useful case
  is the *default* one: recommending something you haven't watched.
- **Two variants share one `ShareCard` shell**, branching on a discriminated `body` union
  rather than living in two components. The frame (gradient, brand header, cover, title, meta
  lines, footer) is then identical by construction rather than by discipline — two exports of
  the same app must not read as two different products. The union also means the fields of the
  variant you aren't rendering are *absent*, not merely unused.
- **`metaLines` is a list the caller breaks**, not a wrapped string. Only the caller knows a
  season *range* plus the length facts overflows one line on a fixed-width card, and CSS
  wrapping would break at an arbitrary `·`.
- **The episode count sits on exactly one line, decided by a single predicate.** The status
  line takes it as watched-of-total whenever the rating records a count; only then does the
  catalog line drop it. So it's never shown twice, and never lost on a rating predating
  per-status episode counts. Encoding this in two places is how it silently duplicates.
- **The info card never drops a fact — it shows `--`.** A missing episode count reads `-- eps`;
  a missing genre/studio/age chip is a `--` chip. Same reasoning as the greyed attribute pills:
  an image travels without the app around it, so "not on record" has to be distinguishable from
  a rendering fault, in a way it doesn't on a page the reader can interrogate.
- **The franchise breakdown is one function with an optional numerator.**
  `franchiseBucketLine(media, ratings | null)` yields the rating card's `3/5 main media` or the
  info card's `5 main media`. One function so the omit-an-empty-bucket rule can't drift —
  neither variant may ever advertise "0/0 recaps".
- **The card shows the whole attribute profile and marks what's unrated** (bare radar web with
  muted labels, `--` pills) where the page hides an empty Attribute Summary entirely. An image
  travels without the app around it, so a missing axis would be indistinguishable from an axis
  that doesn't exist. This is the one place the card deliberately diverges from the page.
- **Not-yet-aired media are invisible to the anime card.** They can't be rated, so counting an
  announced sequel made a fully-watched franchise read as unfinished.
- **A one-entry anime borrows the media-grain wording.** For a film, its own type and runtime
  say more than "1/1 main media". "One entry" is measured over the *aired* set, so an announced
  sequel can't flip a film into the franchise shape.
- **The age-rating chip keeps the page's orange rather than the theme tint** the genres use. In
  one hue it just reads as a seventh genre; the pill is a different kind of fact.
- **Spoiler protection does not gate sharing.** Asking to share is asking to see: the card
  can't spoil a show the viewer just requested a description of. Attempted and reverted — see
  Failed approaches.

### Capture pipeline

- **The cover is inlined to a data URI *before* the capture**, not left to the rasterizer's own
  asset embedding. Two reasons: the capture step then performs no network I/O (its timing stays
  deterministic, which matters because we race a readiness signal against a timeout), and a
  dead cover degrades to the card's "No image" placeholder instead of a blank box.
- **The card renders plain `<span>` chips, not `GenreBadges`/`StudioLinks`.** Three independent
  blockers, and a `interactive={false}` flag would have to switch off all three — at which
  point it selects a different component rather than a mode. `GenreBadges` calls
  `ensureGenresLoaded()` on mount, which would put a `GET /filters/genres` **inside the capture
  window** and break the no-I/O-during-capture invariant; `StudioLinks` renders `<button>`s,
  which would be the first focusable elements inside an `aria-hidden` subtree; and the app
  `Tooltip` is self-contained, so it mounts a provider per chip into the clone. Only the
  *tokens* are shared (`badgeGenreColor`, `badgeAgeRatingColor`, and the airing tints).
- **Each variant's PNG is cached for the dialog's lifetime**, keyed by variant, alongside a
  cover data-URI cache keyed by source URL (the two variants can want different covers — a
  one-entry anime borrows its member's). Toggling is then a swap of already-rendered bytes.
- **A fresh card instance per build** (`cardMounted = false` → `tick()` → `true`). Both
  readiness signals fire once per *mount* — the radar's paint event and the info card's own —
  so re-rendering the existing instance across a variant switch would leave the capture waiting
  on a signal that never comes again.
- **The info card raises its own readiness**, `onMount` → `tick()` → cover `decode()` → one
  rAF, and the dialog settles the cover *before* mount when there's no chart. On the rating
  variant the radar's paint bought the cover hundreds of milliseconds of decode time for free;
  removing the chart removes that accidental buffer, so the decode has to become explicit.
  `onMount` rather than an `$effect` so it runs once by construction and `onReady` never joins
  a dependency set — the same reasoning that puts `EChart`'s `finished` registration outside
  its option effect.
- **`EChart.onReady` is backed by ECharts' own `finished` event**, registered in `onMount`
  beside the click wiring. The first cut fired it after the first `setOption` returned, which
  made "pixels exist" true only if the *caller* also remembered `animation: false` — two files
  agreeing for one signal to be correct, enforced by a comment. It also has to stay out of the
  option `$effect`, or the callback prop joins that effect's dependency set and an inline arrow
  re-triggers a full `notMerge` redraw on every parent update.
- **`AttributeBadges layout="wrap"` for the card.** The orbit's `md:` branch is a *viewport*
  query, so the same rating would export differently per device; and the orbit's pills overhang
  their declared 240px box by ~50px a side, which a fixed-width card has no slack to absorb.
- **`font: false` on the capture.** The app ships no webfonts (Tailwind's system stack), so the
  font-embedding pass has nothing to find. Adding a webfont means turning this back on.
- **The share sheet can't be closed from script.** `navigator.share()` returns a Promise with
  no cancellation path and the Web Share spec has no `AbortSignal` — `AbortError` only ever
  means *the user* dismissed it. So a second click on Share can't take the sheet down; the
  honest behaviour is for the button to go inert (`Sharing…`) while one is up, with
  `InvalidStateError` swallowed as a backstop for platforms that settle the previous promise
  late or never.
- **iOS routes saving through the share sheet**, via a deliberately narrow UA check
  (`isIosLike`). No feature detection exists for the thing that matters: an `<a download>` on
  iOS Safari lands in Files, and only the sheet's "Save Image" reaches Photos. Since both
  outcomes then live behind one picker, the two buttons collapse into one there.

## Failed approaches
- **An `$effect` that read its own output → endless rebuild loop (shipped to the browser).**
  `$effect(() => { if (open) generate(); })` looked like it depended on `open` alone, but
  `generate()` calls `releasePreview()`, which **reads** `previewUrl` before writing it. That
  read put `previewUrl` in the effect's dependency set, so every successful build re-triggered
  the effect forever. Symptom in Safari: "Building your image…" → a red error flash → repeat.
  The error was a *second* symptom, not the cause: an overlapping run entered while the first
  was mid-flight, and the first run's `finally` set `cardMounted = false`, so the overlapping
  run hit `if (!cardEl) throw`. Fixed with `untrack()` around the call plus a `generationId`
  guard (the detail pages' `loadRequestId` idiom). **Learning: in Svelte 5 the self-trigger is
  invisible at the call site** — the offending read is inside a helper two frames down. If an
  effect calls a function that touches the same state it writes, wrap it in `untrack`.
- **A `showEmpty` prop on `AttributeRadar`, added and removed one round later.** Added so the
  card could always draw the radar while the page kept its old "render nothing" behaviour. The
  next round of feedback asked for the page to behave the same way — at which point both call
  sites would pass the prop, making it dead flexibility. **Learning: when a prop exists only to
  preserve one caller's existing behaviour, check whether that behaviour is actually wanted
  before adding the prop.** Removing it also collapsed two readiness paths into one.
- **Two test assertions that could never fail.** `queryByText('Pace:')` was asserting the
  attribute block was absent — but `AttributeBadges` renders `{label}: <strong>{value}</strong>`
  inside one span, so the element's text content is `"Pace: --"` and an *exact* `'Pace:'` query
  returns null whether or not the block rendered. Both "no attributes" tests passed vacuously.
  Caught by a review agent, not by the suite. Now regex-matched.
- **Swapping `if (val)` for `if (isAttrRated(val))` silently lost type narrowing.** The
  truthiness check had been doing the `string | null` → `string` narrowing; the helper returned
  plain `boolean`. Rather than a local `val && isAttrRated(val)`, `isAttrRated` became a type
  predicate — better for all nine callers, and it improves the one negated site.
- **Gating the media share button on the spoiler frontier — designed, built, reverted.** The
  reasoning looked sound (the info card puts the cover and synopsis into the dialog preview,
  which is exactly what the page blurs) and it was an explicit product decision before
  implementation. It failed on contact for two independent reasons. **It couldn't work:**
  `SpoilerGuard`'s click-to-reveal is component-internal state, so the page's `isMediaVisible`
  — the *precomputed* frontier — never flips, and a button disabled by it can never re-enable
  after a reveal. **And it shouldn't exist:** opening the share dialog is a deliberate request
  to have this entry described, so there is nothing to protect the viewer from.
  **Learning: when a feature reads a piece of state to decide something, check that the state
  actually tracks the user action you have in mind.** "Visible" here means "past the computed
  frontier", not "currently on screen unblurred" — two things that read identically at the call
  site.
- **Sizing the synopsis clamp to the common card.** `line-clamp-9` left ~115px of dead space at
  the bottom of a typical card; raising it to 14 filled that but sliced line 13 in half on
  heavy cards, with no ellipsis, because the container's `overflow-hidden` cut in before the
  clamp could. Settled at **12**, sized to the *worst* band (two chip rows, ~245px) rather than
  the common one (~276px). **Learning: with a static clamp inside a bounded flex child, size it
  so the clamp always fires first** — the clamp trims with an ellipsis, the overflow trims
  mid-word. A ~40px gap on common cards is the price of never cutting a heavy one badly.
  The catalog decided the number: a read-only probe found 121 of 279 anime and 306 of 1701
  media exceed the character budget, and the worst combined case (long synopsis + widest chips)
  is Junket Bank / Liar Game / Psyren — which is what the layout was then checked against.

## Gotchas & learnings
- **jsdom "passing" for the wrong reason.** After the radar became unconditional, every
  `share-card.test.ts` test mounted an `EChart` — and passed only because `getEcharts()`'s
  dynamic import lost a race with Testing Library's auto-unmount (`disposed` short-circuits
  before `echarts.init`). One `async` test or a warm transform cache would have flipped it to
  `echarts.init` in a canvas-less, `ResizeObserver`-less environment. Now
  `vi.mock('$lib/echarts', () => ({ getEcharts: () => new Promise(() => {}) }))` makes the
  guarantee structural.
- **`modern-screenshot` re-embeds every non-`data:` `<img>` src as base64 on *each* capture**
  (its fetch cache is per-call). The card originally used `phsar_logo_transparent.png` — 132 KB
  fetched, base64'd to ~176 KB, and re-decoded during rasterization, for a 28px mark. Swapped
  to the 961-byte `favicon-32x32.png`.
- **The foreignObject-safe CSS subset.** `backdrop-filter` and CSS `mask` do not survive the
  clone; `filter: blur()` does. Nothing whose layout depends on the viewport may be used, or
  the export varies per device. The card is constrained to that subset on purpose.
- **Flex lays out the *unrotated* boxes.** A tilted pill adds ±(width · sin θ)/2 of vertical
  reach that the row gap has to absorb — 8° on a ~290px pill needs 20px per side against an
  8px gap, which is why the wrapped rows visibly collided. Fixed with much gentler wrap tilts
  (1.5–3°) plus `gap-y-5`, both carried on the existing `PILL_STYLES` rows.
- **The media page now cold-loads the ECharts chunk (~330 KB gz) on a first share**, because
  the radar always renders and that page mounts no other chart. Inherent to the
  always-show-the-attributes decision. Mitigated by warming the bundle alongside the cover read
  and raising the card-ready ceiling to 15s — and by *correcting* the old comment, which
  claimed the timeout could never fire. It can, and if it does the capture proceeds against a
  half-drawn radar.
- **`formatSeasonRange`'s docstring lied.** It claimed to collapse an equal start/end pair, but
  the body only checked `!end` — so both call sites carried the equality check themselves.
  Fixed while extracting `isSeasonRange`, which now lives beside the formatter it must agree
  with (layout hangs off the two staying consistent).
- **System fonts only means the export is near-identical, not byte-identical, across devices**
  (SF on macOS, Roboto on Android). Accepted rather than embedding a font subset.
- **`text-sm` is 13px here, not Tailwind's 14px** — an `@utility` override in `app.css:155`.
  Every line-count calculation on the card depends on it (13 × 1.5 = a 19.5px line box), so a
  budget worked out from Tailwind defaults is wrong by ~8%. Worth re-deriving rather than
  assuming, since the card has no slack: header 64 + footer 43.5 leaves the white card 535.5px
  of content at 468px wide, and the shipped rating card uses 344 of the 347.5 available below
  the hero row.
- **The card's `overflow-hidden` doesn't clip the overflowing element — it clips the footer.**
  The outer div is a fixed 675px with `overflow-hidden`, so content that overruns doesn't grow
  the card; it bleeds past the rounded white surface and pushes the host + date line out of
  frame. A 5px miscalculation silently costs the branding, which is why the caps are structural
  rather than advisory.
- **`line-clamp` is a prefixed legacy stack the rasterizer may not carry across.** It compiles
  to `display:-webkit-box; -webkit-box-orient:vertical; -webkit-line-clamp:N`, and
  `-webkit-box-orient` is exactly the kind of legacy longhand a computed-style copy can drop —
  in which case the box falls back to *horizontal* and the synopsis renders on **one line**.
  Hence the character cap in the pure builder: cap and clamp guard different failures and both
  are needed. Note the card has only ever been confirmed in Safari, a WebKit engine where
  `-webkit-box` is native; Chrome and Firefox foreignObject passes are unverified for it.
- **Svelte 5's `$state` proxy does not wrap a `File`.** `proxy()` bails on anything whose
  prototype isn't `Object.prototype`/`Array.prototype`, so storing `{file, url}` in `$state`
  proxies the wrapper but leaves the `File` itself intact — `navigator.canShare({files:[file]})`
  and the download anchor get the genuine object. Worth knowing before reaching for
  `$state.raw` defensively.
- **A prop rebuilt as a new object each flush does not re-trigger an effect that only reads a
  string derived from it.** The wrappers hand the dialog freshly-allocated `rating`/`info`
  objects, but the dialog's effect depends on `variant` — a *string* — so Svelte's referential
  `equals` on the intermediate derived stops the propagation and a cached preview can't be
  spuriously rebuilt. The chain is only safe because the intermediate value is a primitive.

## Future work / technical debt
- **Airing-status string literals.** `'Not yet aired'` is hardcoded at ~10 sites and
  `'Currently Airing'` at ~4 more, across 6 files, while `airing_status` is typed as bare
  `string`. A typo fails **silently** — a filter predicate just becomes a no-op. Deserves a
  typed union plus named constants in one pass; deliberately skipped here as cross-cutting and
  unrelated to this feature. `statusBadges` in `shareContent` is now a third copy of the
  four-way cascade (after the two hero pages) and would be the natural place to converge on.
- **The two hero pages still inline their airing-status tints.** The card's copy moved to
  `classes.ts` (`badgeAiring`/`badgeUnaired`/`badgeUpcoming`/`badgeFinished`) as the first
  consumer; migrating `routes/anime/+page.svelte` and `routes/media/+page.svelte` onto the same
  tokens is a one-token swap left for whenever those files are next opened. Until then a
  palette change is a three-file edit with no compiler help — and the card is the copy that
  only ever appears in exported PNGs, so it's the one most likely to be missed.
- **`animeShareContent`/`mediaShareContent` are exported but have no production caller** —
  `animeRatingCard`/`mediaRatingCard` wrap them, and only the tests import them directly. Kept
  deliberately: they're the pure string-rule core, and 17 focused tests read better against
  them than against `.body.statusLine`. Fold them in if the indirection ever costs something.
- **The synopsis clamp can't adapt to the chip rows.** `line-clamp-N` is a compile-time
  constant, so one number has to serve both a one-chip-row card (~276px) and a two-row one
  (~245px); the current 12 leaves ~40px unused on the common case. A derived clamp class picked
  from an estimated row count would close it, at the cost of guessing wrap behaviour from label
  lengths — not worth it without a real complaint.
- **`AttributeBadges` orbit overhang (pre-existing).** The orbit paints roughly 340×228 while
  declaring `width: 240px; height: 200px`. The file's `TODO(v0.15.1)` about pills overlapping
  on narrow viewports is the same defect. Sizing the box to its true painted extent is the real
  fix; `layout="wrap"` is a workaround for the card, not a resolution.
- **No test covers the rasterization itself** — jsdom has no canvas, so the whole
  `captureCardPng` path is unverified by CI. A dev-browser/Playwright smoke test asserting a
  1080×1350 PNG of non-trivial byte size would close the largest untested gap in the feature,
  and would have caught the `$effect` loop.
- **Eliminate the media-page ECharts cold-load** by drawing the card's radar as inline SVG
  (`radarData.avgs` is already normalized 0..1). Cost: a second renderer to keep geometrically
  identical to the page's, against the reuse-the-page's-viz decision above. Cheaper half-step:
  hover/focus-prefetch the chunk from the share button, which needs a prop on `HeroIconButton`.
- **The rating card's hero column is still the tight one.** 176px holding title + up to two
  meta lines + the score circle (~156px computed); a long CJK title filling the 2-line clamp
  *plus* a season range is the case to check. The info card's column was re-derived and fits
  (170.5px worst case) — which is why its title pins `text-lg` and its badges use `py-0.5`
  rather than the hero page's `py-1`; at the larger sizes it clips.
- **Clipboard copy** (`ClipboardItem` with `image/png`) was scoped out. Worth revisiting for
  desktop paste into WhatsApp Web / Discord / Slack, where there's no native share sheet.
- **An opt-in "include my note" toggle** was scoped out (privacy). Revisit only on request.
- **The bucket labels are card copy living in `shareContent`** rather than with the relation
  sets — fine while there's one consumer. (They singularize as of `3472f24`.)
- **The dialog's `reset()` runs twice on close** when the viewer toggled away from the default
  variant: `reset()` clears `picked`, which changes the effect's tracked `variant`, which
  re-enters the closed branch. It converges immediately (the second pass is a no-op) and costs
  microseconds, so it's left alone — noted so the next reader doesn't mistake it for the
  self-trigger loop under Failed approaches, which was unbounded.
- **The cover cache is cleared on dialog close**, so a share → close → share again re-fetches
  and re-base64s the cover (~100KB per encode). Hoisting the `Map` to module scope would fix it
  — cover URLs are immutable catalog data — at the cost of unbounded growth across a session.
- **Font determinism.** Embedding one subset woff2 would make exports byte-comparable across
  devices, at the cost of turning `font: false` back on and paying the embed per capture.
