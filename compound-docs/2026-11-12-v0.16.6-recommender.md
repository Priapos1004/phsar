## Recommender

The full agreed design is the session plan (2026-11-13); what follows is what a later
session couldn't reconstruct: the numbers behind each choice, the alternatives that lost,
and the traps. Users are "the heavier" (351 rated anime, 68 natural anime events) and "the
lighter" (259, 28) — the repo is public, so no ids or titles tied to a person.

### Measured (dev DB = prod restore 2026-11-12; 19 prod dumps 2026-07-21 → 11-11)

Read-only analyses run by subagents; every ranking number is out of sample (time split
and leave-one-user-out over 88 decisions, ~1,700 candidates each, random hit@25 0.015).

- **Bulk imports**: 298 anime over 3 days (2,686 h of watch time) and 238 in one day
  (2,107 h); natural days never above 17 h.
- **Taste vs next pick**: galaxy kNN (K=20) + MAL predicts the user's own score at r 0.73 /
  0.61 (kNN alone 0.64 / 0.52, MAL alone 0.66 / 0.56) — but ranks the next pick *worse than
  random* (median ~1,000 of ~1,600). Similarity to the last 10 natural picks: median 70 /
  64, hit@25 0.29 / 0.25.
- **Bake-off** — pooled conditional logit (53 features, L2) vs "recent similarity + near
  own top 20%": hit@25 0.33–0.34 vs 0.11–0.21, median rank 70–77 vs 96–115, liked-hit@25
  0.31–0.32 vs 0.05–0.16 (paired p 0.02–0.05). The lift is continuations (0.61 vs 0.00)
  and this-or-last-season shows (0.57 vs 0.14); back-catalogue picks stay at 0.13–0.20 for
  every model. Families that carry it: lag matches (F2), release-age/freshness propensity
  (F8), top-20 proximity + votes (F5); continuation (F7) and plain recent similarity (F1)
  are redundant once F2 is in.
- **Enjoyment tilt** (score + α·z(kNN)): α 0.5 neutral on hits, slightly up on liked hits;
  α ≥ 1 lowers both. The enjoyment predictors predict ratings (ρ kNN 0.50, MAL 0.69) but the
  average pick sits 0.34 SD *below* its candidates on kNN.
- **Watchlist** (rebuilt by diffing dumps): caught 75% / 44% of natural picks; lift P1 19×,
  P2 4.4×, P3 1.2× (≈ unlisted); entries ≤ 30 d old 8×, > 90 d 1.9×; conversion hazard
  falls ~3× after 30 days; 3 removals without a watch in 114 days (nobody prunes); a
  priority raise preceded a watch within a week in 5 of 14 cases. Ordering by priority then
  newest alone: median rank 27, hit@25 0.47 for the heavier user.
- **List size**: per-entry odds ~35× on the small list vs ~15× on the big one, capture 0.38
  → 0.77 — between users only; within one user γ in `a + γ·log(backlog)` can't be pinned
  (pooled −0.69 [−1.52, +0.19]).
- **Freshness**: 0–30 d after a season's finale 15–18× over availability, 31–90 d 3–4×,
  > 2 y 0.4–0.5×; "currently airing" 0.5–1.2× — users binge at or after the finale.
  Continuations are fresher still.
- **Commitment size** (back-to-back events of one anime merged into a run): 4–6 h runs
  2.3–2.9× over availability, ≥ 10 h ~0.1×; 17 of 18 runs finish everything aired;
  planned vs actual run hours ρ 0.92–0.96.
- **Rewatches**: 15% / 32% of natural runs. 10 of 24 led into the franchise's next media
  within 0–7 days (57× / 18× over other completed anime) — a refresh before continuing, not
  before it airs. Complete rewatches with nothing new rank poorly (best median 59 of 315,
  hit@10 0.15–0.18 vs random 0.03–0.04).

### Decisions and the alternatives that lost

- **A personal search sort `for_you`, not a `/recommendations` endpoint.** A carousel is a
  saved search, so the sort brings see-all, filter-set toggles and Open in search for free,
  and sorts any search "for you". The endpoint would have needed a bespoke row and could not
  sort a search.
- **One stored ranking per user, recomputed lazily on read** (an inputs key over ratings,
  watchlist, watchlist events, the live fit, the history override, the version and the UTC
  date). No stored candidate pool: scoring all ~1,600 candidates costs milliseconds. Not a
  job — a per-user job counts against `JOBS_PER_USER_LIMIT`, shows in the bell and waits
  behind sweeps. Not on write — it would add latency to every rating and watchlist edit.
- **Pooled weights, committed after review**, not per-user fits: per-user weights lost to
  pooled in every split (inner CV chose pooled; forcing them cost 3–8 points of hit@25).
  Per-user behaviour enters through propensity features and the calibrated slate.
- **One Home row with per-user calibrated caps** (each source's share ≈ the user's own pick
  mix, shrunk toward the pooled mix), not separate "Up next" / "Recommended" rows — the user
  wants the single row to carry the balance.
- **Auto-detected history start + a settings override**, not a manual date only: the rule
  credits watch time per day away, so a holiday catch-up stays natural, and catches later
  imports too; the override covers slow imports volume can't see.
- **Event time is the completion moment** (first watch event, else the rating's
  `created_at`): five on_hold → completed ratings moved by 1–4 days, order unchanged.
- **Commitment length counts listed media**, because users list earlier seasons to rewatch
  beside a new one; a ≥ 20 h media stands alone (the readiness rule's constant).
- **Watchlist event log by trigger**, not service writes: a log that misses a path corrupts
  training data silently. **Exposure stretches**, not a top-50 append per recompute, which
  grows with every rating, edit and day.
- **The harness as a weekly prod job** whose `result_summary` is read on the job page — not
  an admin card, and not a log file (the container filesystem is wiped on redeploy).
- **No MAL crowd recommendations** — the galaxy already answers "if you liked X".
- **"Recommend hot anime" is a setting, not learned.** Ratings land at the finale, so a
  weekly watcher's history looks like a finale-week binge: the data cannot separate someone
  who wants nothing hot from someone who follows shows weekly.
  - "Hot" is the watchlist's own verdict, ported to the backend (readiness.md's "second
    surface"). A first cut used "finale over a week ahead". The user pointed out the verdict
    covers more — a franchise season due next season, unlisted sequels, the dropped-season
    and standalone exemptions — and is the word they already see.
  - Both implementations walk one JSON case table (`frontend/src/tests/fixtures/readiness-cases.json`)
    rather than two copies that agree.
  - A Waiting anime (nothing to watch until a sequel lands) is never a candidate.

### Failed approaches

- **Taste as the ranking** (the first sketch's "neighbours of what you rated highly",
  weighted by score): predicts ratings, not picks — worse than random at the next pick.
- **Temporal rhythm features** — due-ness per facet (events or days since the user last
  picked a sector / region / genre / length vs their usual gap), trends (recent share vs
  older), watch intensity: due-ness hurt significantly out of sample, trends hurt in the
  time split, intensity did nothing.
- **Per-granularity momentum / alternation**: nothing survives correction for genre,
  region, sector, studio, length or demographic; power needs ~270 events for genre, ~1,000
  for sector. The one sequential effect is franchise binges (every repeat-anime move back
  to back).
- **Inner-CV family selection** dropped the most useful family (F2) in all 4 outer splits:
  50–80 training decisions can't choose among families.
- **"I rewatch because I run out of material"**: fresh supply at rewatch time was equal or
  higher; no lull, no point-in-quarter effect.
- **Long-anime due-ness**: 3 long runs (≥ 10 h) in 91 natural runs — unmeasurable.

### Gotchas

- **pgvector reads go through text** (`register_vector` is never called): 2k × 515
  features parse in ~240 ms on the event loop; `cast(features, ARRAY(REAL))` ~20 ms.
- **`galaxy_compute.distances` puts inf on its diagonal**, so a lag feature must count a
  continuation of the lag pick as *most* similar explicitly.
- **BaseModel's uuid default runs once per statement for `INSERT … SELECT`**, so a bulk
  replace via `unnest` gives every row the same uuid — use executemany.
- **Merges and splits move media between anime** without touching rating or watchlist rows:
  the inputs key needs `sum(media.anime_id)`. Fit ids repeat after a restore + refit: key on
  the fit's uuid.
- **An old frontend crashes on an unknown sort** (`SORT_OPTIONS.find(...)!`), so the
  release deploys both images on one tag, frontend not after backend.
- **A trigger logging watchlist deletes must skip cascades** from an account or media
  deletion, or the log's FK fails the account deletion.
- **Import-day rewatch events** (21 logged within minutes of the first watch) mean "watched
  more than once before", not when.

### The compute core (where it departs from the bake-off's spec)

`recommender_compute` ports the bake-off's 53 features exactly (the subagent's spec, with
its λ=10 reference weights as the stand-in `WEIGHTS` until the backtest refits), except
where the agreed design overrides it — so the refit, not the reference, is what ships:

- **An on-hold season is story left.** The spec counted any rated media as watched, which
  dropped `on_hold` anime from the candidates; the parity test against `_rated_state`
  caught it. Candidates are now exactly the rated states none / in_progress / on_hold.
- **A listed anime's commitment is its listed media**, rewatch seasons included, and a
  ≥ 20 h media stands alone (the readiness constant, pinned to `watchlistReady.ts`).
- **New families, unfitted until the refit**: `F9` (length propensity), `W_*` (watchlist
  priority, entry age, backlog) and `F10`, freshness by the commitment's *finale* — the
  spec's `F8` buckets by season *start*, which lumps a show still airing with one that
  finished last week, while the measured preference is 0.5–1.2× airing vs 15–18× within 30
  days of the finale. A finale more than a week ahead is its own bucket; MAL's
  missing end dates are estimated at a week per episode.
- **Anime and media facts come from their app owners**, not the bake-off's own derivations: the
  card's MAL score and votes (the bake-off took the main-story mean and the *max* votes), the
  genre majority rule (the bake-off took genres on at least half the main story), the main
  story's studios, `SEASON_KEY`, `Media.total_watch_time`, and the watchlist's
  `franchise_signals` for readiness. One more reason the refit, not the reference, ships.
- **Readiness for an unlisted anime reads its whole main story as though listed** (the user's
  rule): an airing or next-season main-story media makes it Hot through B1, which has no
  dropped or standalone exemption.
- **Dropped from the design**: spreading a catch-up day's events over the gap. No shipped
  family reads event times finely enough for it to matter.
- **Smoke run on the dev DB** (2026-11-13): load 0.36–0.44 s (catalogue + history), compute
  0.01–0.08 s per user. A user with no ratings gets popular picks with three
  new-territory slots.

### The fitting harness and the first fit (2026-11-13)

`recommender_eval` replays every natural pick and fits the conditional logit (full softmax,
λ·Σw², L-BFGS-B from zero — the bake-off's spec); `scripts/backtest_recommender.py --fit
--dumps ../backups_prod` produced the committed `WEIGHTS`. Analyses ran in subagents.

**Measured** (dev DB = prod restore 2026-11-12; the watchlist rebuilt from the backups from
2026-07-21; 88 decisions, 64 + 24, ~1,680 candidates each; λ = 10):

| | time split: logit / score / row hit@25 | liked-hit@25 (logit) | median rank (logit) | NLL | LOUO: logit / score / row | liked | median | NLL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| refit | .583 / .556 / .583 | .474 | 23 | 4.872 | .443 / .398 / .364 | .469 | 28 | 5.369 |
| bake-off reference weights (in-sample) | .417 / .444 / .472 | .368 | 30 | 5.337 | .398 / .409 / .409 | .388 | 36.5 | 5.549 |
| similarity baseline | .111 | .053 | 118 | 6.315 | .159 | .082 | 99.5 | 6.318 |

- **Port fidelity**: the pick's mean standardised value per feature is within .11 of the
  bake-off's on all 51 shared features (within .066 with the readiness gate off). The gaps
  are the deliberate departures: the genre majority rule (F2 genre Jaccard ±.06) and the
  standalone split of the commitment (F6 +.05). The baseline reproduces the bake-off's
  (.111 at median 115 there).
- **Well above the bake-off's .33–.34**, because of families it lacked. Ablations
  (Δhit@25 / ΔNLL, p on the NLL; time split | LOUO): W +.111 / +.430 (.013) | +.034 / +.324
  (< .001); F10 +.167 / +.268 (.013) | −.011 / +.093 (.031); F5 +.056 / +.143 (.009) | +.023 /
  +.119 (.005); F6 +.041 (.015) | +.017 (.07) on the NLL. F1, F2, F7, F8 and F9 are within
  noise once the others are in (F8 is mostly absorbed by F10). Harness-only, as additions: F3
  −.038 | −.155 (.001), F4 −.062 (.033) | −.011, F6 interactions ≈ 0.
- **`W_backlog`** passes the both-splits rule: +.139 (p .046) | +.189 (p < .001); weight
  +.754, sign-stable in all 4 outer fits. With two users it is largely a between-user term
  (how much each picks from their list), not a within-user backlog effect.
- **λ**: 3 / 10 / 30 give LOUO NLL 5.41 / 5.37 / 5.57 — 10 stays.
- **Per-user weights** still lose: time-split NLL 5.26 (λu 10) and 4.98 (λu 100) against 4.87
  pooled.
- **Sign stability**: 12 of the 40 weights flip sign in some outer fold; of the 15 largest,
  all agree in 4 of 4 folds except `F7_log_days_since` (3 of 4).
- **The enjoyment tilt now costs hits**: α = 0.5 takes hit@25 from .583 to .556 (time split)
  and .443 to .398 (LOUO), and LOUO liked-hit@25 from .469 to .429. α stays at 0.5 for the
  exposure log to retune.
- **Watchlist metrics** (76 picks from 2026-07-21): listed at the time .83 (heavier) / .35
  (lighter), .68 pooled. Lift over random choice among the candidates: P1 29×, P2 8.3×, P3
  2.1×, unlisted .34×; entries ≤ 7 d 22.5×, 8–30 d 11.4×, 31–90 d 5×, > 90 d 3.7× (2 picks).
  Hazard per 100 listed-anime-days: P1 .68, P2 .19, P3 .05; .33 within 30 days against .13
  after (2.5×). Higher than the earlier dumps study (P1 19×, ≤ 30 d 8×), because this
  rebuild keeps an entry listed until right after its rating when the rating falls between
  two backups, and lift is measured against the gated candidates.
- **Runtime**: the decisions ~9 s, every fit together ~8 s; `--fit --dumps` 26 s wall.

**Decisions** (the user's calls, 2026-11-13):

- **The watchlist's features stay raw**, not standardised per decision. A list holds 7–8 of
  ~1,750 candidates for the lighter user, and an indicator with k positives among n
  standardises to ≈ √(n/k), 15–40: listed anime gained several logits, and LOUO NLL was 6.25
  (8.04 for the lighter user) — worse than the reference weights. Raw: 5.37 (5.43). Hits
  moved within noise (LOUO .483 → .443, liked .500 → .469; time split .583 both). Only raw
  can `W_backlog` mean anything: standardised, `on_list · log B` is ±z(listed) again, and it
  failed the rule (+.008 | −.001).
- **The readiness verdict reads the listed media only once something listed has aired**,
  else the main story. Listing only an unannounced sequel made the anime Waiting and dropped
  a real continuation pick: 1 of 88 on the backups' history, 7 of 88 with today's list
  replayed. The candidate rule already treated such an anime as unlisted. Accepted cost: the
  watchlist page shows it Waiting while the recommender offers its aired season.
- **The list is unknown before `listed_from`** and reads as empty. Fitted from today's list
  alone, every W weight came out negative (p1 −.12 … backlog −.28): an entry still listed
  today is by construction one not yet picked. Now a fit without history leaves W at 0, and
  the weekly eval's refit can't report a confident wrong sign before the event log covers
  the past.
- **`W_backlog` ships only if removing it raises held-out NLL in both splits.**
- **hit@25 is judged on the logit** (α = 0) for the bake-off comparison, with the score and
  the row reported beside it.
- **The complete-rewatch family moves to the weekly eval job**: it needs every watch event,
  not just the first, and no shipped weight depends on it.
- **λ is fixed at 10** (the bake-off's inner CV chose it in every outer split), and the time
  split keeps the bake-off's definition (other users' later decisions train too), so the
  numbers compare.

**Gotchas**:

- **Picks are timed at the finish**, so a list edit made during the watch counts as prior
  intent: 2 of the lighter user's 8 listed picks were listed or raised under 2 minutes
  before the rating, and one of their lists works as a "watching" status (81× lift).
- **The backups miss entries listed and removed between two of them**, and a removal is
  dated to the rating it follows (+1 s) or else to the first backup without it.

### Fewer weights (2026-11-13)

The user asked whether 40 four-decimal weights on 88 picks overfit, and whether weights
should be tuned per user each week. A subagent measured it on the same 88 decisions (deltas
are held-out NLL against the 40-weight λ = 10 fit, time split | LOUO; p paired sign-flip):

- **Not harmful overfitting**: more shrinkage hurts — λ 30 / 100 / 300: +.19 / +.57 / +1.19 |
  +.20 / +.66 / +1.22 (p < .01). Rounding to 2 decimals changes nothing (+.002 | +.006); the
  median bootstrap SE of a weight is .085, so the third and fourth decimals were noise.
- **But ~17 weights carry no signal.** One block per family — `F1_sim_decay10`, the lag-1
  `F2` matches, `F5`, `F6`, `F7_is_cont`, `F8`, `F9`, `F10`, `W` (23 weights) — matches the
  40: −.032 (CI [−.18, +.13]) | −.005 ([−.12, +.13]), the tightest intervals of any variant.
  Hit@25 .556 / .443, liked .526 / .429. 19 of 23 weights sign-stable across the 4 outer
  folds (28 of 40 before); replayed as weekly refits over the last 10 weeks, 0–2 near-zero
  sign flips a week, no weight moving more than .13, 24–25 of the top 25 kept.
- **Smaller still falls short**: `F5`+`F6`+`F10`+`W` (13 weights) +.13 | +.19 (LOUO p .08);
  adding `F7` or `F1_sim_decay10` doesn't close it.
- **Learning curve**: training on 25 / 50 / 75 / 100% of each fold, NLL 5.43 / 5.22 / 4.96 /
  4.87 (time split), 5.95 / 5.77 / 5.53 / 5.37 (LOUO) — still falling, so more picks help.
- **Per-user weights lose** at every pull toward the pooled fit (λu 3 … 1000: +.69 … +.02),
  and from scratch (+.55, p .001), all of it through the lighter user (14 training picks).
  The heavier user's own-only fit would tie the pooled one at roughly 55–65 own picks.
- **λ chosen by random-fold CV picks 1**, which overfits across users (+.40 LOUO, p .003):
  λ stays fixed.

**Decisions** (the user's calls):

- **23 weights ship, at 2 decimals**; the other 17 stay computed and are measured as
  additions. Refit NLL 4.840 / 5.364; `W_backlog` still passes the both-splits rule (+.143,
  p .042 | +.204, p < .001).
- **No per-user weights** until a user has ~40–60 natural picks; the harness keeps testing a
  pull toward the pooled fit.
- **A weekly refit applied automatically behind guardrails** (to build with the weekly eval
  job), not "measures and never applies": weights in a DB table, `WEIGHTS` the fallback; held
  back when held-out NLL worsens beyond noise or a weight above 0.2 flips. Features still
  ship by commit.

### The user's review of their own row (2026-11-14)

Generated for the heavier user from the dev DB (23 weights, today's list): 5 of the first 10
were anime they had considered watching next, and they watched one of them next. Three fixes
came out of it:

- **A hot anime with nothing to start now waits.** The #1 was a finished first season listed
  again beside its sequel due next season. The readiness verdict tests "airing or due" before
  "anything to watch", so it read Hot, and Hot passes while "Recommend hot anime" is on — but
  all it offered was the rewatch, two months early, where refresh rewatches come 0–7 days
  before the sequel. Test A now applies whatever the verdict (8 such candidates left the
  heavier user's set); the watchlist keeps its Hot.
- **The explore slots weigh the user's own length propensity** (`F9`), not a ban: they had
  filled with 24–146 h series (runs ≥ 10 h are picked ~0.1× their availability). The user
  asked for per-user rather than a fixed penalty, so someone who watches long series isn't
  pushed off them. After: 5–15 h picks.
- **A continuation reads "continue"**: the watchlist group's raw features outweighed the
  continuation flag, so listed continuations read "watchlist" — which the tile's bookmark
  already says. The user's call: show the rarer, more informative reason.

The refit moved one weight by its last digit (`F10` .44 → .43). A small score change moves
listed anime across the cap's edge, so ranks near it shift more than the weights do.

### Storage and serving (2026-11-14)

**Decisions** (the user's calls unless noted):

- **The two logs and the state row on `BaseModel`**, only the `recommendations` cache on bare
  `Base` — superseding the plan's "logs on bare `Base`". `BaseModel`'s `created_at` is what the
  watchlist log needed anyway: seed rows carry the migration's `created_at`, so the log's start
  (`listed_from`) is `min(created_at)`. Lost: an extra `logged_at` column (mine), and seed rows
  dated at the migration, which would have made every old entry look freshly listed for months.
- **Seeding as found**: `added` at the entry's `created_at`, `changed` at its `modified_at` when
  later — both with the entry's current list, the only one known.
- **The trigger merges churn**, shaped over a table of cases:
  - window **3 days** — 1 day merges too little ("removed yesterday, re-added for now"), 1 hour
    lets churn grow, no limit erases real absences;
  - the merged event is **dated at the latest write** ("when it first stood at P1");
  - a rating or watch **from 3 hours before the earlier event on** blocks it: "watched, removed
    on the rating card, re-listed for a rewatch" must stay three events, since a watch and the
    removal it caused belong together. The first draft blocked only on a rating after the
    last event, and merged that case away.
  - no grace rule for removals: the rating card removes the entry in a request after the
    rating's, so the log dates the removal after the pick.
- **Both grains** for `for_you` and `recommended` — a description search ("training arc")
  sorted for you is a real use. A media sits at its anime's rank, its anime's media in watching
  order (over top-rated: a sequel shouldn't lead its first season), through
  `SEASON_KEY` then MAL id (`for_you_key`), pinned to `chronological_media_key` by a test;
  unranked media keep the sort's top-rated tiebreak. `recommended=false`
  works too, mirroring `watchlisted` (mine).
- **Dev DB migrated** (2026-11-14): 619 entries seeded 654 events (35 edited since listed);
  inside a rolled-back transaction a real entry's P2 → P1 logged one `changed`, and P1 → P2
  cancelled it.

**Gotchas**:

- **A `UNION` of string literals types them as `text`**: the seed's enum labels need a cast.
- **An AFTER trigger fired by a cascade runs after its parent is gone**: without the existence
  check the logged row's foreign key fails the account deletion.
- **A trigger's `WHEN` can't read `OLD` on an INSERT**, so the "priority and list unchanged"
  guard sits in the function rather than on a separate UPDATE trigger.
- **A test is one transaction, so `now()` never moves**: the trigger tests age events by
  moving their `at`, and a rating "two hours ago" sets its `modified_at`.
- **The first concurrency test passed without the lock**: with no state row yet, the two
  requests already serialise on the `INSERT … ON CONFLICT` unique key. The test now starts
  from a stale stored ranking, where only the row lock holds the second request back.
- **ORM bulk UPDATE by primary key refuses a WHERE clause**: extending exposure stretches with
  executemany goes through the Core table.
- **The review's cuts** (`/simplify`): the key tracks the watchlist through its log alone
  (merges set `modified_at`; note edits stay out), drops the fit's uuid (a refit replaces every
  star, so their newest id moves), and scans each table once; `last_at` went, since it always
  equalled `modified_at`; the catalogue and ledger build moved into the compute thread.

### Future work

- "Not interested" on tiles (roadmap); per-list (tag) weights once the event log has
  history; a complete-rewatch slot when the weekly eval flags it; the γ backlog term once a
  single user's history pins it; a "watching" list as its own signal; α retuned from the

## Side work: the backend test suite on its own database, and a faster CI job

### Where it started (measured 2026-11-11)

- **Local full run: 18 min 25 s** (1105 s, 1338 tests) against the dev DB, a restored prod
  dump: 1964 anime, 6724 media, 862 ratings.
- **CI job ~8–10 min.** Step times of one run: containers 21 s, torch 17 s, other deps
  27 s, pg17 client 8 s, schema + seed 18 s (mostly the uncached HF model download),
  mypy 41 s, **tests 325 s** (sum of per-test times from the log: 311 s over 1336 tests).
- **Local hotspots, all from sharing the dev DB:**
  - `test_reembed_regenerates_existing_vectors_in_place` — 303 s: `reembed_all_embeddings`
    re-encoded every row in the DB (~9k encodes at ~30 ms) to check three fixture rows.
  - ~45 backup tests — ~370 s: a real `pg_dump` of the whole catalogue, ~5 s each;
    `test_retention_pins_current_dump_against_eviction` alone did 16 dumps, 79.5 s.
  - `test_a_pass_cut_short_leaves_the_probe_stale[anime]` — 30 s: walked ~7k media and
    rating rows (a savepoint and a `get` each) before reaching the anime.

### Decisions

- **Own empty DB by default, dev DB as an opt-in** (`PHSAR_TEST_DB=dev`) rather than one or
  the other. The dev DB finds bugs only realistic data shows; the default run shouldn't
  pay ~12 min for it, and the running dev server's `JobWorker` polls the dev DB and can
  claim jobs the tests queue.
- **Rebuilt every session** rather than reused with a schema fingerprint: ~0.6 s, and rows a
  test committed and failed to clean up (the `jobs_id_seq` bump past 1e9, say) never carry
  over. The dev DB's sequences now stay put; every run used to advance them.
- **Built by replaying the migrations**, not `create_all` + a stamped version row. ~0.3 s
  more per session for the schema prod actually runs (enum types, server defaults,
  indexes). It also let `alembic check` move into the suite (`tests/test_migrations.py`),
  so CI's separate `migrationcheck` database and step went, and a local run catches
  model/migration drift too.
- **Retargeting in `tests/__init__.py`**, not at the top of `conftest.py`: Python runs a
  package's `__init__` before any module in it, so `settings.DB_NAME` is renamed before
  anything imports `app.core.db` (which builds its engine from settings once) — with no
  E402 `noqa` pile in conftest.
- **The drop guard reads `engine.url.database`**, and requires it to equal
  `settings.DB_NAME` and end in `_test`: it guards the database every test connects to,
  so an import that built the engine before the rename is refused rather than dropped.
- **Router auth: `auth_service.create_user` + `create_user_access_token`**, shared by
  registration, the user seeder and the fixtures, rather than a test-only copy of the
  side effects. A per-user default added later reaches all three. The HTTP flow stays
  for `test_auth.py` and the registration-defaults test. A real argon2 hash is cached per
  password, so minted users can still log in and confirm an account deletion.
- **Backup tests skip under the dev opt-in** (in the `backup_dir` fixture) rather than a
  stubbed `pg_dump` in ~25 logic-only tests: nothing in the dev data changes what they
  check, and the default run and CI still run them all.
- **Seasonal-sweep windows open on the DB clock** (`clock_timestamp()`) rather than
  selecting children by `parent_job_id`: the flake was two clocks compared with a ~2 ms
  margin; the identity-based fix touched nine call sites for a collision only the dev
  opt-in could produce.
- **xdist with a database per worker**, `<DB_NAME>_gwN_test` — the worker id goes *before*
  `_test` because the guard drops nothing else. `--dist loadfile` lives in `pytest.ini`
  so a bare `pytest -n 4` can't silently fall back to per-test distribution and rebuild
  the galaxy fit on every worker. The dev opt-in refuses `-n`: fixed usernames would
  block each other's inserts, and the backup staging table and dedupe would collide.
- **CI runs `-n 3`, not `-n auto`**: locally a worker per core (8) was slower than half that,
  because Postgres shares the cores. The runner has 4 vCPUs plus the Postgres container.
  Unverified on CI.
- **CI caches the installed venv, not pip's download cache** — unpacking the wheels
  (torch's above all) is most of what an install costs. The HF cache keys on the service
  file with a prefix fallback, so a dependency bump doesn't re-download ~470 MB; mypy's
  keys on `app/**` (all mypy checks) with a prefix fallback.

### Failed approaches

- **Cheaper argon2 in tests** saved only 0.07 s of 0.37 s. A verify costs what the *stored*
  hash's parameters say, so the seeded admin's login stayed full cost — and lower context
  parameters made `needs_rehash` true, adding a re-hash and an UPDATE to every admin
  login. The cost was the round-trips, not the hashing.
- **`alembic stamp` in-process** to write the version row: `env.py`'s `fileConfig` defaults
  to `disable_existing_loggers=True`, silently muting every app logger already imported for
  the whole run. The suite stayed green only because most modules import during
  collection, after the build. A file-less `Config()` avoided it but dropped every other
  `alembic.ini` option; the fix kept is the real `alembic.ini` with
  `config.attributes["configure_logger"] = False`, which `env.py` now honours.
- **`xdist.is_xdist_controller`** for "skip the build on the controller": it tests
  `dist != "no"`, so once `pytest.ini` sets `--dist loadfile` a plain serial run counts as
  the controller and builds nothing. The check kept is `WORKER or not config.getoption("tx")`.
- **Module-level skip markers on the backup test files**: over-skipped tests that never
  dump. Moved into the `backup_dir` fixture, which every dumping test takes.
- **`-n auto`** (a worker per core): slower than half the cores, see above.

### Gotchas

- The `db_session` fixture's connection is already in a transaction, so SQLAlchemy 2.0's
  join mode turns an app `db.commit()` into a no-op persistence-wise — "API commits
  persist to the dev DB" (an old note in `test_admin_completion.py`) was never true.
- Docker Desktop's VM clock ran ~0.6 ms behind the host: any `created_at >= python_now`
  window can miss rows the database stamps a moment later.
- The xdist controller loads the root conftest even for a bare `pytest -n 4`; anything
  heavy imported there is paid before any worker starts.
- numba builds its DEBUG dumps lazily, only when the logger is enabled for DEBUG — so
  capping the `numba` logger at WARNING removes the cost, not just the output.
- ruff's isort treats `alembic` as first-party because `phsar/alembic/` exists, and splits
  `from alembic import …` from `from alembic.config import …` across groups.
- `test_job_worker.py::test_notify_wakes_idle_worker` timed out once in a full run (5 s
  wait). It passed 5 runs alone, two services-only runs and the full rerun, and the
  test DB held no leftover queued jobs. Its own comment says 2 s flaked under load
  before. Left as is.
- The review pipeline's cost on these blocks was mostly prose: each block's docstrings
  repeated a reason another file owned (`maintenance.restores`, `create_user`), or listed
  members that would go stale. Fix shape every time: one owner states the reason, the
  rest point at it.
